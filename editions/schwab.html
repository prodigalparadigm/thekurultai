What this document is

Schwab is hiring a Director, Responsible AI in San Francisco: requisition 2026-127806, category Risk & Regulatory, pay range $248,300 to $370,000 a year plus bonus eligibility, application deadline 3 November 2026.

This is what the Kurultai produces from the public record alone. Schwab brought nothing to the instrument.

It is also the edition where the instrument finds the least to correct, and that is the point of including it. Schwab has done the thing most organizations get wrong. Reading how they did it is more useful than watching someone fail.

The unbundling, in their own words

Most organizations hiring their first AI leader write one posting that contains five jobs. Schwab wrote five postings.

Their careers page describes the team: "Schwab's AI Strategy & Transformation team, known as AI.x, is the central hub for Artificial Intelligence at Schwab. We are an integrated product, engineering, research, risk, and enablement team focused on advancing AI responsibly across the enterprise."

Product, engineering, research, risk, enablement. Five functions, named. And the board shows them staffed separately rather than described aspirationally - these were live on Schwab's own board on 1 November:

Alongside them, Senior Manager AI Risk and Supervision, Senior Manager AI Supervision & Controls, and Director Internal Audit AI Technology. This is a hiring programme, not a hire.

Which is the finding, and it is not a criticism. The split is correct, and it costs five salaries. The organizations that most need the split are the ones that cannot buy it, and they respond by writing one job description containing all five functions and hiring one person to fail at them. What Schwab purchased with headcount, a smaller organization has to purchase with clarity: knowing which of the five it actually needs first, which it already has in the building, and which can wait. That is the staffing map, and it is the reason this instrument exists.

What the posting gets right

It bounds the scope: responsible AI strategy "for client-facing AI solutions," not for everything. It names the partner functions rather than leaving collaboration to hope - "legal, risk, compliance, product, engineering, and research teams." And it asks for operational governance rather than principles, preferring experience "operationalizing responsible AI development practices through governance processes, risk assessments, model documentation, testing, monitoring, and issue escalation."

One detail deserves naming because it is true across this whole industry and almost never said out loud. At a ceiling of $370,000, the requirement is eight or more years in trust and safety, responsible AI, risk or a related field, with at least two focused on AI systems, and three or more building AI risk frameworks. Even at this level, with this budget, nobody can require more than two years of focused AI-systems experience, because the field is not old enough to have produced it. Any organization writing a job description that demands a decade of generative-AI expertise is describing a person who does not exist.

Engine one: the ceremony audit, outside-in

Instrumental,
and unusually concrete

The 10-K's model-risk governance is the real thing, and it covers AI explicitly:

"Schwab manages model risk, including use of artificial intelligence, through use of policies, standards, and controls which evaluate the conceptual and technical soundness of models used by the Company. Prior to the use of any artificial intelligence, we employ procedures which require cross-functional review from applicable oversight functions. We maintain a model inventory that includes a distinct record and risk rating for each model, and we conduct independent validations, annual reviews, and performance monitoring of the Company's models."

A model inventory with a risk rating per model, cross-functional review before any AI is used, independent validation. That is a control judged by whether it governs an outcome, not a statement of values. FY2025 Form 10-K, Management's Discussion and Analysis, accession 0000316709-26-000009.

The gap: governed,
but never described

Here is what the documents do not do. Across 623,636 characters, the 10-K uses the phrase "artificial intelligence" five times: twice as a threat (the sophistication of fraudsters and attackers), once in a forward-looking bullet listing "industry and competitive trends including artificial intelligence, digital assets, private company securities," and twice in the model-risk passage above. It contains no occurrence of machine learning, generative, algorithm or data science. There is no account of AI as a capability Schwab is building.

The 2026 proxy statement - the document where management tells shareholders what it is paid to care about - mentions artificial intelligence zero times in 348,634 characters. No AI, no machine learning, no algorithm.

So: a named enterprise AI organization, hiring across five functions at up to $370,000, called in Schwab's own words "the central hub for Artificial Intelligence at Schwab" - and the shareholder-facing record governs AI as a model-risk control and otherwise does not mention it.

This is not a scandal and nothing here suggests concealment. Filings are conservative by construction and a risk framing is the safe one. But it is a genuine gap with a cost: the investment is real, the governance is real, and the story that connects them exists only on a careers page. A capability nobody has described to the people funding it is a capability that has to be re-justified every budget cycle.

Engine three: the people arc

The posting asks the Director to "translate emerging AI risks, regulatory expectations, and enterprise standards into practical guidance for teams building client-facing solutions." Translation is the whole job, and translation fails silently when the receiving teams have not written down what they actually do.

The arc is the same at this scale as at any other. The teams named in the posting document their own work in their own words, see where AI already touches it, and the standards land inside those workflows instead of arriving as a policy nobody can apply. At Schwab's size the risk is not that the guidance is wrong; it is that it is correct and unread.

Engine four: the staffing map

Schwab's AI.x buys all five archetypes outright. Read against the map: the AI Engineer and Site Reliability roles are Builders and Maintainers, the Researcher is a Prototyper, the Platform Product Manager is a Grower, and the Director of Responsible AI is a Sweeper in the most literal sense - the person who finds what the others missed before a client does.

An organization that cannot buy five directors is not thereby excused from needing five functions. It is required to sequence them. Which one first depends on where the work actually is, which is what the ceremony audit establishes and what no org chart can tell you in advance.

The ninety days, outside-in

By day 30

Connect the governance to the story. The model-risk paragraph in the MD&A is strong and nearly invisible; the AI.x description is vivid and lives on a careers page. One of these audiences votes on the budget. Naming the capability in the shareholder record costs a paragraph.

By day 60

The written escalation path for the Responsible AI function: what happens when it says no and delivery says yes, named role to named role. Cheap now, retroactively impossible.

By day 90

The first decision-ledger review, with the freed capacity from AI-assisted operations traced to an assigned choice rather than absorbed.

A note on the front door, for completeness rather than emphasis: schwab.com has no llms.txt and no AI-crawler policy in either direction, though its homepage does carry Organization structured data and working Open Graph. It is in better order than most. For a firm whose clients increasingly ask an assistant before they ask an adviser, the absent piece is a stated position rather than a repair.

The commitment this document honors

The Kurultai does not produce headcount-reduction recommendations, ever. Nothing in this edition proposes one. Schwab's own documented AI value sits in governance, fraud prevention and client capability, and these recommendations follow that line, because that is where the evidence is.
