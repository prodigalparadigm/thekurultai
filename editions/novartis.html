What this document is

Novartis is hiring an Executive Director, NovaOS Strategy and Innovation - AI: requisition REQ-10086325, posted 24 August 2026, $236,600 to $439,400 plus performance cash incentive and possible annual equity, based at East Hanover, New Jersey with a distant arrangement possible.

It is the best-shaped AI leadership posting in this library, and it is the one whose organization's own annual report never mentions what it is for.

What the posting gets right

Candor first, because this posting earns it. It asks for a strategist to do strategy: "10+ years of experience in corporate strategy, product strategy, innovation, management consulting, or digital transformation leadership," and only a "solid understanding of AI, enterprise software, digital platforms, and product management principles." It does not demand fifteen years of engineering for a job about choosing what to build.

The platform is named - NovaOS. The partner functions are named: "Product Management, Applied AI, Engineering, UX, and Commercial teams," which tells you delivery lives elsewhere and this seat is not secretly responsible for it. Governance is the deliverable rather than the garnish: "Establish innovation governance, portfolio prioritization, and investment frameworks that balance short-term delivery with long-term strategic growth." And the role is expected to "build and lead a high-performing strategy and innovation organization" rather than to be one.

Against Applied Materials elsewhere in this library - five responsibility blocks, one chair, reporting into engineering - this is the same industry moment answered with a structure instead of a bundle.

Engine one: the ceremony audit, outside-in

Instrumental: a risk
pointed the other way

The 20-F carries a dedicated entry whose framing is unlike anything else in this library:

"Artificial Intelligence. Risk description: Failure to successfully implement AI solutions and capture the full potential of AI opportunities... If we fail to drive an effective enterprise AI strategy and capture these opportunities, or if our competitors secure more sophisticated AI models, algorithms or partnerships than us, the result will be significant loss of competitive advantage in developing and launching our medicines."

It goes on to name flawed algorithms, bias, hallucination and ethical exposure - so this is not naive optimism. But the headline risk is not moving. Most organizations' AI risk entries describe what AI might do to them. This one describes what will happen if they fail to use it. FY2025 Form 20-F, accession 0001114448-26-000004.

The gap: the vehicle
for a declared risk

The annual report says failure to drive an effective enterprise AI strategy would cost competitive advantage in developing and launching medicines. The enterprise AI platform built to drive that strategy is called NovaOS.

"NovaOS" appears zero times in the 20-F. So do "enterprise AI platform" and "AI platform", across 1,011,526 characters.

The honest qualifier belongs here rather than in a footnote: the 20-F reports the group, the posting sits in Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corporation, the US affiliate, and a platform may simply fall below the disclosure threshold. Nothing here suggests concealment. The finding is narrower and still real - the named vehicle for a declared strategic risk is invisible to the people reading about the risk, and a capability that shareholders cannot see is one that has to be re-argued internally every planning cycle.

Ceremonial: the
filing cabinet

At the foot of the posting, in Novartis' own classification: Business Unit, General Management. Functional Area, Marketing.

The Executive Director who will set the strategy for the enterprise AI platform is filed under Marketing. The reporting line is not stated anywhere on the page.

This is the quietest finding in the library and possibly the most useful. Where an organization files a role is a statement about what it believes the work is, made in a dropdown, before anyone is hired, by someone who was not trying to say anything. Across this library the same work is filed three ways: Risk & Regulatory at Schwab, Information Technology at Applied Materials, Marketing here. One of those organizations is going to be surprised by what it hired.

Engine three: the people arc

This posting is unusual in asking the hire to "build and lead a high-performing strategy and innovation organization," which means the people arc is part of the job rather than a consequence of it. The risk in a platform strategy role is that the strategy lands as a roadmap and never as a changed working day.

The named partner functions - Product Management, Applied AI, Engineering, UX, Commercial - are where that gets decided. They document what they actually do, see where NovaOS already touches it, and the roadmap is written against observed work rather than against a market scan. In a regulated industry this is not optional: a capability nobody can describe is a capability nobody can validate.

Engine four: the staffing map

This seat is a Grower, cleanly: ecosystem partnerships, portfolio prioritization, moving validated concepts into roadmaps, building the organization behind it. There is a Prototyper element in "strategic discovery, concept development... and incubation of new AI-enabled capabilities," and the posting is explicit that Builders and Maintainers live elsewhere, in Applied AI and Engineering.

That is what a correctly scoped AI leadership hire looks like, and it is why this edition spends less time on the posting than the others do. The map's open question is not inside this role. It is whether the Sweeper exists anywhere - the person who finds what the platform got wrong before a regulator does, in a company whose own risk entry names hallucination and bias.

The ninety days, outside-in

By day 30

State the reporting line, in writing, before the offer. A strategy seat with no declared line and a Marketing classification will be read by the organization as marketing, whatever the job description says.

By day 60

Decide whether NovaOS is group infrastructure or a US-affiliate platform, and say so somewhere a shareholder can read it. The risk is already disclosed; the answer to it is not.

By day 90

The first decision-ledger review, with the trial-execution and yield gains the 20-F anticipates traced to an assigned choice rather than absorbed.

A note on scope, stated rather than buried: this edition reads the Form 20-F and the posting. Novartis also publishes a Swiss Annual Report and a Compensation Report under Swiss ordinance, which is where executive compensation lives since no proxy statement exists in this regime. Neither has been read here. Any finding about what Novartis does or does not disclose is a finding about the 20-F, not about Novartis' reporting as a whole - including the absence of EU AI Act language, which may well appear in the Swiss documents.

The commitment this document honors

The Kurultai does not produce headcount-reduction recommendations, ever. Nothing here proposes one. Novartis' own documented AI opportunity sits in trial execution, yield and discovery - capability, not subtraction - and these recommendations follow that line, because that is where the evidence is.
