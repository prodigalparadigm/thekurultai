What this document is

Applied Materials is hiring a Senior Director, Enterprise AI & Automation in Santa Clara: job ID R2620304, posted 23 September 2026, $224,000 to $308,000.

This is what the Kurultai produces from the public record alone. Applied brought nothing to the instrument.

The finding here is not that anyone has been careless. It is that the company's own documents describe AI as a top-tier enterprise risk under board oversight, and the organization's answer to it is one chair in the engineering department.

Engine one: the ceremony audit, outside-in

Instrumental:
the risk inventory

The 10-K carries a dedicated AI risk factor, and it is a careful one. Under the heading "We are exposed to risks related to the use of AI by us and our competitors," it enumerates flawed algorithms and biased datasets, costly implementation, dependence on third-party AI providers, the possibility that "our competitors may be more successful in their AI strategy," and emerging regulation. Elsewhere it names AI and deepfakes as an attack vector against its own systems.

That is not boilerplate. It is a company that has thought about what could go wrong. FY2025 Form 10-K, accession 0001628280-25-056742.

And it reaches
the board

The proxy confirms the risk is governed at the top. "Our most recent ERM assessment identified cybersecurity, geopolitical environment, AI, trade and global regulatory environment, and talent scalability and succession as among the top risks for the Company." The board's own described role includes "overseeing long-term strategy, including geopolitics and AI-related opportunities and risks."

AI is a named top-tier enterprise risk with explicit board attention. DEF 14A, accession 0001193125-26-027307.

A note against overreading: most AI references in the proxy concern AI as market demand - the semiconductor buildout Applied sells into - rather than Applied's own adoption. The two lines above are the exceptions, and they are the ones that matter here.

Ceremonial: the word
"strategy" in the title

Now the posting. The role is classified on Applied's own board under category Information Technology, and the position overview states the reporting line exactly: "Reporting to the VP of Engineering, this leader will own the full AI/GenAI lifecycle."

Its responsibilities arrive under five named headers, which is the document's own structure and not a reading imposed on it: Strategic AI & GenAI Leadership; Enterprise LLM Platform & Multi-Cloud AI Infrastructure; MLOps & Model Lifecycle Management; Agentic AI & Automation; Robotic Process Automation. One of those blocks carries a target of "measurable cost avoidance ($100M+ annually)."

And the qualification that settles it: "Contributions to AI governance frameworks, responsible AI policies, or AI ethics programs" appears under Preferred. The governance answer to a board-level risk is a nice-to-have on a seat that also runs the RPA centre of excellence.

The word "strategy" in the title is the ceremonial layer. The requirement for fifteen years of software engineering, and the reporting line into engineering, describe what the job is. Veblen's test is not whether a thing is useful but whether it is judged by whether it controls a future outcome; an enterprise AI strategy filed under delivery is judged by delivery.

What the market is paying

Set beside another edition in this library: Charles Schwab is hiring a Director of Responsible AI at $248,300 to $370,000, classified under Risk & Regulatory, scoped to that one function. Applied is hiring five functions, classified under Information Technology, at $224,000 to $308,000.

The market is pricing the bundle below the split. From a risk standpoint that is backwards: the seat carrying more surface area, less specialist depth and a $100M delivery target is the cheaper one. This is not a criticism of either compensation decision. It is the clearest available evidence that the industry has not yet agreed what this work is worth, because it has not yet agreed what it is.

Engine three: the people arc

The posting asks this leader to build "a culture of innovation and responsible AI use" across Contract Analytics, Quality, Finance and Supply Chain, while simultaneously running MLOps pipelines and an RPA estate. Culture work and platform work compete for the same hours, and platform work has deadlines.

The arc that survives this is the ordinary one. The four named domains document what they actually do, which is also the only way to know whether an agent belongs there; the governance arrives inside those workflows rather than as a policy issued from engineering. Without it, adoption is measured in licences rather than in work changed.

Engine four: the staffing map

Read against the five archetypes, this single posting asks for all five at once. Prototyper, for the "strategic discovery" and incubation. Builder, for the multi-agent systems and the LLM platform. Sweeper, for model governance, bias detection and drift. Grower, for enterprise adoption across four domains. Maintainer, for the RPA estate and the CDSW-to-Databricks migration.

No individual is all five, and the market has priced that correctly elsewhere in this library. The question the map puts to Applied is not which candidate to hire but which archetype this seat is actually for, and where the other four already live in a company of this size. They almost certainly exist. They are simply not named together.

The ninety days, outside-in

By day 30

Name the operational owner of the AI risk the board is already overseeing, and say whether that is this seat. If it is, move responsible AI out of Preferred and into the job. If it is not, the board's risk has no operator.

By day 60

Decide which of the five responsibility blocks this role keeps. The other four do not disappear; they get owners or they get dropped on purpose rather than by attrition.

By day 90

The first decision-ledger review, with the avoided cost from automation traced to an assigned choice rather than absorbed into the baseline.

The commitment this document honors

The Kurultai does not produce headcount-reduction recommendations, ever. Nothing here proposes one, and that is worth saying plainly on an edition about a company whose posting carries a $100M cost-avoidance target: avoided cost and removed people are not the same thing, and the ledger above exists precisely so the difference stays a decision somebody makes on purpose.
