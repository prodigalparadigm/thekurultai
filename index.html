A freed hour with no owner is not a saving.

This is the part most strategy documents skip. Work gets faster, nobody decides where the time goes, and it is quietly absorbed — the same work, at the same pace, with the slack gone. The Kurultai · Engine one

The ceremony audit.

Point an instrumental tool at a ceremonial process and the process absorbs it and produces more ceremony, faster.

What it does

Every process you describe is sorted into two piles. Instrumental: it is judged by whether it controls a future outcome. Ceremonial: it runs on precedent, or it displays status.

The rule

Recommendations attach only to the first pile. The second pile is named for the management conversation it deserves, with no tooling recommendation attached to it.

What is The Kurultai?

A free AI strategy tool from Prodigal Paradigm. You describe your organization's processes in your own words and place yourself on four sliders; it returns a strategy document built from that and nothing else.

Who is it for?

Organizations deciding what to do about AI and considering hiring someone to tell them: companies, nonprofits, government bodies. It is aimed at the moment before the ill-scoped AI-strategist hire, not after it.

Will it recommend layoffs?

No. The Kurultai does not produce headcount-reduction recommendations, ever. That promise is enforced twice: in the engine's instructions, and again in code on the way out, where a breach gets one corrective retry and then fails the job rather than returning the document. The test battery behind it includes a buyer who sits down and asks for the layoff plan outright. The enforcement is in the public repository, because "enforced, not hoped" is only checkable if you can read it.

What happens to what I type?

It is sent to Anthropic's API to be read, and comes back as your document. Nothing is stored on our side but a count, so the day's limit can be enforced, and the finished document, which deletes itself within two hours. No account, no tracking, no record you did not take with you.

What does it cost?

The QUICK sketch is free and needs no account. The DETAILED document and the advisory session are priced on request, because the scope depends on what you are deciding; write and ask.

What do the four engines do?

The ceremony audit sorts your processes into the ones judged by whether they control a future outcome and the ones that run on precedent, and attaches recommendations only to the first kind. The management-decision ledger assigns every freed hour to a named role with a date. The people arc documents what people actually do before deciding what the job is now. The staffing map places you against five builder archetypes and names where hiring enters. They are described above and shown working in a sample document.

What is MyRunbook?

Each person writes down what they actually do all day, in their own words. Not a process diagram drawn by somebody else - the work as the person doing it understands it. It is the only way to see which parts of a job hand off to a machine and which cannot, and it is how knowledge that currently lives in one person's head gets onto a page before that person leaves.

What is MyWork?

What follows MyRunbook. Once the work is visible on a page, the person and their manager decide what the job is now: what it keeps, what it sheds, and what it gains. MyRunbook is the observation; MyWork is the decision. Doing the second without the first is guessing.

What are the five archetypes?

Boris Cherny, Head of Claude Code at Anthropic, proposed five team archetypes that transcend traditional job titles - engineer, designer, product manager - in the age of AI (reviewed November 2, 2026). The staffing map reads your organization against them, because a seat advertised as one title is usually asking for two or three of these at once, and that is the thing worth naming before anybody is hired.

Prototyper. Generates brand-new ideas and churns out many concepts quickly, most of which never ship.

Builder. Takes a prototype or rough idea and rapidly turns it into a production-grade product or infrastructure.

Sweeper. Cleans up the interface, simplifies code and systems, unships failing features, and optimizes performance.

Grower. Takes an existing shipped product and iterates continuously to improve product-market fit.

Maintainer. Owns mature systems and keeps them secure, reliable, fast and efficient as they scale.

The mix moves with your product stage. Before product-market fit the weight sits on Prototyper, Builder and Sweeper; growing, it moves to Builder, Sweeper, Grower and some Maintainer; once the thing is working, to Sweeper, Grower, Maintainer and some Builder. No individual is all five, and a posting that asks for all five is the finding, not the candidate.

How is this different from an AI maturity assessment?

A maturity assessment scores you. This does not: there is no score, no grade and no right end of any slider. It also refuses to recommend anything it cannot be judged on, so every recommendation names the future outcome it controls and the evidence that would show it.
